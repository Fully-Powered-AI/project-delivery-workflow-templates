# Project lessons review

When a phase or a project ends, the project manager gets one blameless review: whether it achieved what it was for, how scope, schedule and budget moved and why, whether the success measures were the right ones, and practice changes for the team to choose, each with an owner and a follow-up date.

*Capability template · Project and delivery · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/project-lessons-review/)*

## The outcome

The next project starts better than the last one finished. The review sets each result beside the aim it was meant to serve and gives a plain verdict, and it says so when a result is not in yet instead of guessing. Every day of delay and every amount over budget is traced to a recorded change, or shown openly as untraced, so the team discusses facts rather than impressions.

It looks hard at the measures too. A measure that only counted activity, one with no level agreed in advance as a miss, or an aim nobody measured at all, is the lesson that makes the next plan sharper.

The lessons become practice changes with teeth: each one names the step, the point in a project where it applies and the role that carries it out, with an owner, a follow-up date and what will show it was used. Changes from the last review are checked, so a lesson learned twice is noticed. Nobody is judged: the review speaks about practices and roles, and remarks about a person are left out. The project manager and the delivery leads choose every change; the AI assistant chooses nothing and sends nothing.

## Who starts the work, and when

The project manager, or a project coordinator on their behalf, runs it once a phase or a project has ended, ideally within a month while memories are fresh, after a short lessons session with the delivery leads. It runs again when a result that was still due arrives. It suits internal projects and client delivery alike: a new system, an office move, a service launch, a client implementation.

## What they supply

- **A short review brief:** the aims the work was for, numbered; the planned and actual finish; the baseline budget and the actual cost.
- **The deliverables list,** exported from the project tracker: what was in the original scope and what happened to each.
- **The change log:** every recorded change with its effect on days and cost, its reason and who approved it.
- **The success measures:** each with its aim, starting value, target, the level that would count as a miss, and the result or the date it is due.
- **The lessons notes:** what each delivery lead says worked, what did not, and the changes they propose.
- **The changes chosen at the last review,** if there was one.

## What comes out

One draft review in a fixed order: the outcome against the aims with a verdict; scope, schedule and budget, with every movement split into approved changes, changes recorded without an approval, and what no change explains; a look at the measures themselves; what worked, to keep; the practice changes to choose; the earlier changes followed up; anything referred elsewhere; and the questions only the project manager can answer. Empty sections say so in a word.

A vague lesson such as "keep everyone in the loop" is turned into a proposed wording that names the step, the moment and the role, with the original words shown beside it so the team can accept, change or drop it.

## What has to change for your company

Write numbered aims into every project charter, and give every success measure its aim, a starting value, a target and a level agreed in advance as a miss. Keep a change log that records each change's effect on days and cost and who approved it; the review is only as clear as that log. Decide whether you count calendar or working days. Map your tracker's export headings once. Decide where chosen changes live, such as a project playbook, and who keeps it. For incidents, launches and lost deals, use [Incident investigation and corrective actions](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/incident-investigation-and-corrective-actions/), [Launch checklist](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/launch-checklist/) and [Win-loss review](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/win-loss-review/); while a project is still running, use [Project status report](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/project-status-report/). It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from files and notes you supply; it needs no connection to your project software.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
