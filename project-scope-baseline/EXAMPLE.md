# Project scope baseline: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The firm, its people, the vendor, the project and every date and figure below are invented. The firm is an invented 60-person professional services firm putting a client document portal in place. The sponsor is the managing partner and the project manager is the operations manager; people are named by role only, and the vendor is called Vendor A. First come the four inputs the operations manager supplied, in full, then the draft the workflow template returned. No working deliverable list existed, so none was supplied.

## The inputs

### 1. Settings

```
Project: Client document portal
Sponsor: Managing partner
Project manager: Operations manager
Date: Thursday 12 November 2026
```

### 2. The approved brief

```
CLIENT DOCUMENT PORTAL: PROJECT BRIEF (approved Monday 2 November 2026)

Why: Clients send documents by email and ask us where their work stands.
Staff spend too much time chasing documents. One place for both fixes this.

What the project delivers:
1. A client portal on Vendor A's platform where each client can upload
   documents and see a status for each of their open engagements.
2. The open-engagement folders (140 today) moved from the shared drive
   into the portal.
3. A one-page guide for clients on how to use the portal.
4. Training for the 35 staff who work with clients.

Not in this project: integration with the billing system; a mobile app.

Targets: go-live on Monday 1 March 2027. Total budget $48,000.
Success: clients find it easy to use, and document-chasing emails drop
significantly.
```

### 3. Discovery notes and constraints

```
DISCOVERY NOTES, Monday 9 November 2026 (operations manager with two team leads)
- Team leads want clients to be able to send messages to staff inside the portal.
- Closed engagements from the last three years, about 600 folders, must stay
  searchable by staff. Nobody has decided where they will live.
- Vendor A's standard plan includes 50 staff seats. The firm has 60 staff.

CONSTRAINTS (operations manager, Tuesday 10 November 2026)
- The $48,000 budget includes the first year of Vendor A's fees, quoted at $18,000.
- The operations manager is away from Monday 21 December 2026 to Monday 4 January 2027.
- No staff training can be held from Monday 18 to Friday 22 January 2027 (firm retreat).
```

### 4. The sponsor's expectations

```
From a conversation with the managing partner, Tuesday 10 November 2026:
- "Clients should be able to pay their invoices there too."
- "Leave the shared drive alone for now."
- "Send me a one-paragraph progress note every Friday."
```

## What to notice

The brief names four deliverables, and the draft lists exactly those four. Three further things reached the project outside the brief: payment of invoices, messaging and the closed folders. None became a deliverable and none was dropped; each is a question. The sponsor's remark about the shared drive pulls against the brief, so both statements are printed together as a conflict and the remark is also listed as an exclusion marked as not from the brief. The remark about invoice payment is not the same as the brief's exclusion of billing-system integration, so it is a question about an implied deliverable that quotes the exclusion beside it. The $30,000 left after the vendor's fees is the only sum, and it is shown with its working; the seat comparison in question 9 sets 35 and 60 against 50 and adds no figure of its own.
