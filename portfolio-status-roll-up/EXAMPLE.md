# Project portfolio status roll-up: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The company, its people, its projects, its suppliers and every date and figure below are invented. The company is an invented 130-person maker of commercial kitchen equipment running five internal projects at once. The head of operations runs the roll-up each fortnight for the leadership team. People are named by role only. First come the five inputs the head of operations supplied, in full, then the draft the workflow template returned.

## The inputs

### 1. Settings

```
Name: Internal projects
Head of delivery role: Head of operations
Reader: Leadership team
Meeting: Tuesday 10 November 2026
Period: Monday 26 October to Friday 6 November 2026 (fortnightly)
Report date: Monday 9 November 2026
Page limit: default (800 words)
Status word list: none
```

### 2. The project register (exported from the company's project list)

```
Project ID,Project,Lead,Sponsor,Planned end,Depends on
P1,New ERP system,IT manager,Finance director,2027-02-01,
P2,Second assembly line,Plant engineer,Plant manager,2027-01-15,P4
P3,Dealer portal,Marketing manager,Sales director,2026-12-04,P1
P4,Warehouse racking and move,Logistics manager,Plant manager,2026-11-27,
P5,Quality system recertification,Quality manager,Managing director,2027-03-31,
```

### 3. The status reports, as each lead sent them

Four reports arrived. The quality manager sent none for P5.

```
P1 NEW ERP SYSTEM, fortnightly report from the IT manager
Status: AMBER.
Why: cleansing of the customer and price data is two weeks behind
plan, because the ERP supplier moved its consultant to another client
for a week. (Frankly the consultant they sent was not up to it.)
Done this fortnight: chart of accounts mapped and signed off by the
finance director on 28 October; test system live on 3 November.
Customer and price data will be ready for the dealer portal team from
7 December (was 23 November).
Go-live: 1 February 2027 holds if the cleansing finishes by
4 December.
Decision for leadership: approve six extra days of the supplier's
consultant at $1,200 a day ($7,200), so the cleansing finishes by
4 December. Without them it finishes on 18 December and go-live moves
to 1 March 2027. Needed by Friday 13 November. I recommend approving
the days.
```

```
P2 SECOND ASSEMBLY LINE, from the Plant engineer
Status: GREEN.
Done: the four assembly machines arrived on 30 October and passed the
delivery inspection on 2 November.
Next: installation starts on 1 December in the old warehouse bay, once
the warehouse move has cleared it. Commissioning on track for
15 January.
```

```
Dealer portal update (P3), from the Marketing manager
On track. Design signed off by the sales director on 30 October. Build
started on 2 November. We connect to the ERP's customer and price data
from 23 November, as agreed with IT, and launch on 4 December.
```

```
P4 WAREHOUSE RACKING AND MOVE, from the Logistics manager
RED.
Why: the racking supplier has moved installation from 16 to
30 November because its steel delivery is late.
Done: floor marking finished; the fire inspection of the new bay
passed on 2 November.
The move out of the old bay therefore finishes on 11 December, not

Decision for leadership: rent temporary storage for December at
$4,500, or hold the second line's incoming parts at their supplier
until January. Without one or the other, parts arriving from
30 November have nowhere to go. Needed by Monday 16 November. I
recommend renting the storage.
```

### 4. The last roll-up (sent on Monday 26 October, for 12 to 23 October, shortened here to what carries over)

```
P1 New ERP system: AMBER. Reason: data cleansing behind plan.
P2 Second assembly line: AMBER. Reason: machines held at the port;
  delivery expected 30 October.
P3 Dealer portal: GREEN. Reason: design on schedule.
P4 Warehouse racking and move: GREEN. Reason: supplier confirmed
  installation for 16 November.
P5 Quality system recertification: GREEN. Reason: internal audit plan
  agreed.
Decisions asked of leadership:
D1 (P3, asked by the Marketing manager). Should the portal take card
  payments only, or card and account credit? Needed by 30 October.
D2 (P1, asked by the IT manager). Should sales staff stop editing
  customer records until go-live (a data freeze), and from when?
  Needed by 6 November. Options given: freeze from 16 November;
  freeze from 7 December. Recommendation: IT manager: freeze from
  16 November.
```

### 5. The leadership decision notes since the last roll-up

```
- Friday 30 October: the leadership team chose card and account
  credit for the dealer portal (D1).
```

Every status on the page is the one its lead wrote. P2's green stays green even though its own report and P4's report disagree about the old bay: the disagreement is shown as C2 and the plant engineer is asked, but the roll-up does not decide whether green still holds. P2's report lists what arrived and what comes next without saying why the project is green, so the reason reads "no reason given", and because the colour changed from amber, that is a gap. "On track" is not a colour and the settings carry no status word list, so P3 is shown in the lead's own words. P5 sent nothing, so its green from 26 October is shown only as last period's status.

The counts add up: one red (P4), one amber (P1), one green (P2), one not stated as a colour (P3) and one not reported (P5) make the five projects in the register. D1 is answered in the decision notes, so it moves to "Answered". D2 is not in the notes, so it is carried with its number; it was needed by 6 November, before the report date of 9 November, so it is past the date needed. The new decisions continue from D2 as D3 and D4, and the three are ordered 6, 13 and 16 November. Six days at $1,200 is $7,200, as the IT manager's report states. Both contradictions sit on pairs the register links through "Depends on": P3 depends on P1, and P2 depends on P4. The remark about the supplier's consultant is left out of P1's reason and noted for the head of operations. The page, from the title line to the last gap, comes to 800 words, exactly at the 800-word limit, with every project, decision, contradiction and gap kept. The head of operations chases the five people, confirms the statuses, removes the last part and takes the page to the meeting.
