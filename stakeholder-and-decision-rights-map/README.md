# Stakeholder and decision rights map

Before kickoff, the project manager holds one map of everyone the project touches: each group placed by influence and interest, every key decision with exactly one accountable owner, the decision makers still missing, and who hears what, when and from whom. The sponsor confirms every decision right.

*Capability template · Project and delivery · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/stakeholder-and-decision-rights-map/)*

## The outcome

A project that crosses teams starts with its people and its decisions on one page instead of in someone's head. Every group whose work, results or systems the project touches is listed, with what changes for them and what they control. Each is placed by influence and interest, using rules tied to those facts, so the people who can stop the project and the people whose day changes most are both easy to see.

Every key decision gets one accountable owner, never two and never a blank. Where the list falls short, the map says so plainly: a decision nobody owns, a decision two managers both think is theirs, an owner who leaves before the date, a standard decision such as spending beyond the budget that nobody has claimed. Each gap arrives as a question for the sponsor, with the facts beside it, weeks before it could stall the work.

Then the communication plan: who is told what, by whom, on which date, and who is asked for their view in time for it to count. The map speaks about roles and what people have actually said, never about anyone's character. The project manager confirms the decision rights with the sponsor and each owner; the AI assistant names no owner and sends nothing.

## Who starts the work, and when

The project manager runs it in the weeks before kickoff, once the sponsor has agreed the project and there is a first list of who is affected and which decisions lie ahead. It suits any project that crosses teams or changes someone's daily work: a new system, a new site, a new way of handling orders, two teams becoming one. It runs again when a phase ends, when a role on the map changes hands, or when the teams are reorganised.

## What they supply

- **The project brief:** what changes and for whom, the sponsor, the phases and their end dates, the test window and the go-live date.
- **The stakeholder list,** as a spreadsheet: each role or group, how many people, how much changes for them, what they control, and who passes news to them. The project manager may add their own placement where they know something the facts do not show.
- **The decision list:** each key decision with the date it is needed and who is proposed as accountable, responsible, consulted and informed.
- **The project manager's notes:** concerns a group has raised, roles that are leaving or away, anything else known.
- **The settings:** working days, closed days, the weekly meeting day and the channel for each kind of group.

## What comes out

One draft map in six parts: everyone affected, with a check of eight kinds of stakeholder a project easily forgets; the placement by influence and interest, with any difference from the project manager's own view shown for confirmation; the decision chart, each problem flagged where it stands; the missing decision makers, as questions for the sponsor; a dated communication plan, one line for each group; and the open questions for the project manager. It ends with the date of the next review and a line for the sponsor's confirmation.

## What has to change for your company

Use your own column names, mapped once to the defaults. Decide the channel for each kind of group, how many working days ahead people are asked for their view, and how soon they hear an outcome. Add to the five standard decisions any your company always needs, such as approving a new supplier. Add a coverage question for anyone who must sign off a change in your business, such as a safety officer or a works council. Agree who confirms the map and where the confirmed copy lives. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from files and notes you supply; it needs no connection to your project or messaging tools.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
